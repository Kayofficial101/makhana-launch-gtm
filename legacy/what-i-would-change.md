# What I would change in my first makhana launch plan

I made the original appendix early in college. Keeping it here is useful because it shows where the logic improved, not just how the slides improved.

## Arithmetic and definition problems

| Original line | Problem | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| ₹99 MRP, ₹64.35 pre-tax realization, “39% realization” | ₹64.35 is 65% of MRP, not 39%. Tax and promotion were not bridged. | Start with actual selling price, remove GST, then show every deduction separately. |
| ₹33 COGS and “66% gross margin” | The percentage uses MRP even though the brand does not keep MRP. | On the stated numbers, ₹31.35 is 31.7% of MRP and 48.7% of ₹64.35 realization. |
| D2C bundle at ₹599 and ₹84 contribution | Pack count, tax, fulfilment and payment fees were not shown. | Model the bundle by number of packs and show each cost line. |
| Salaries “about ₹5 lakh” | The listed roles add to ₹4.7 lakh. | Use ₹4.7 lakh or explain the missing ₹0.3 lakh. |
| ₹3.5 crore raise | 18 months of ₹19.3 lakh burn is ₹3.474 crore, leaving almost nothing for the separate inventory and product allocation. | Tie each tranche to the monthly cash model and stage gates. |
| 45% of funds for growth | ₹1.575 crore over 18 months is ₹8.75 lakh per month, below the separate ₹12 lakh monthly marketing line. | Reconcile allocation and monthly plan. |

## Assumptions presented as facts

- 35% platform deduction was called standard, although actual commercial terms vary and are private.
- 15 packs per store per day had no pilot evidence.
- The move from 50 to 1,000 stores was calendar-driven, not gate-driven.
- ₹33 product cost had no bill of materials or supplier quote.
- A month 18 to 20 break-even date was stated without a monthly cash schedule.
- The downside break-even comparison used annual average volume, which hid the low-volume opening months.

## Strategic gaps

The document did not define the eating occasion, test taste against a control, measure willingness to pay or explain why a buyer would reorder. “Gen Z” stood in for segmentation. Ghost Pepper, Wasabi, Discord and gaming communities were ideas, but no evidence connected them to the intended customer.

It also treated quick commerce as the whole strategy. There was no store-level test, working-capital plan, expiry rule, product quality specification or explanation of how D2C would work beyond an assumed subscription.

## What I kept

- Asset-light manufacturing is still sensible for a pilot.
- Quick commerce is relevant to an impulse snack.
- A small SKU set is better than a broad launch range.
- Scenario analysis is the right instinct.

## What replaced the weak parts

- a target occasion instead of a demographic label
- primary research materials instead of invented consumer certainty
- a blind taste and price test before inventory
- a two-pack architecture anchored to observed competitors
- channel-specific economics with editable ranges
- a 12-week, ₹30 lakh pilot instead of an immediate ₹3.5 crore launch
- explicit stop, change and scale rules
- quality, label, shelf-life and recall readiness
