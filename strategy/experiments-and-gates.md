# Experiments and gates

The purpose of the launch is to buy evidence in the cheapest sensible order.

| Question | Method | Sample | Metric | Decision rule |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Which occasion is real? | Structured interviews across buyer, lapsed and non-buyer groups | 60 | Repeated occasion and alternative | Continue only if a specific occasion appears across groups |
| Can the product compete on taste? | Blind sequential monadic test against one established control | 120+ | Overall liking and defect reasons | Concept reaches parity; no common texture defect |
| Which core price can work? | Monadic price cells at ₹129, ₹149 and ₹169 | 20 per cell minimum | Top-two purchase intent and alternative named | Select only after economics and intent are read together |
| Does sampling convert? | Unique code by office/community session | 8-10 sessions | Purchase within 7 days | Keep only locations and scripts that clear the pre-set hurdle |
| Is velocity organic? | One controlled promotion then full-price window | 40-60 nodes | Units/node/week at promo and full price | Median 5 units/week by week 4 without deep discount |
| Do buyers return? | Identifiable D2C and platform cohort where data is available | 8-week cohort | Repeat within 8 weeks | At least 20% in the intended segment |
| Does the next order make money? | Cohort contribution calculation | By channel | Second-order CM2 | Positive in the base commercial case |
| Is inventory safe? | Lot and stock-age report | Every batch | Sell-through with remaining shelf life | 70% sold with 60% shelf life remaining |

## Interpretation rules

- A social post is not a demand test.
- A discounted first order is not proof of willingness to pay.
- Average velocity can hide weak nodes, so use the median and distribution.
- Survey purchase intent is weaker than an observed paid order.
- Repeat must be read by acquisition source and discount level.
- A gate can be changed before a test, not after seeing an inconvenient result.
