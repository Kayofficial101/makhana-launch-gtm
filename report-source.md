# Makhana snack launch GTM

## Recommendation

I would fund a **12-week Delhi NCR pilot**, capped at ₹30 lakh, and hold back the rest. The launch would begin with two familiar flavours and two pack sizes. It would not begin with 1,000 dark stores, five adventurous flavours or a national media plan.

The job of the pilot is to answer four questions:

1. Does the product win a blind taste test against an established option?
2. Will the intended buyer pay the planned price without a deep discount?
3. Does a meaningful share reorder within eight weeks?
4. Is the second order contribution-positive after trade terms, promotion and fulfilment?

If those answers are not clear, the right move is to change the product or stop. Distribution cannot repair a weak product.

## What changed from my first attempt

My college version had the right instinct about an asset-light launch and the usefulness of quick commerce. The model underneath it was too confident.

It assumed a 60 g pack at ₹99, 15 units per store per day, a 35% channel deduction and a smooth ramp to 1,000 stores. None of those inputs had a contract, test or source behind them. The revenue bridge also mixed incompatible definitions: ₹64.35 of pre-tax realization on ₹99 is 65%, not the stated 39%. The 66% gross margin used MRP as the denominator and ignored the channel deduction. On the stated numbers, gross contribution was closer to 31.7% of MRP or 48.7% of net realization.

There was a second problem. The ₹3.5 crore raise was almost exactly 18 months of the listed ₹19.3 lakh monthly burn. It therefore could not also support the promised inventory and product allocation. The launch needed gates, working capital and uncertainty ranges. It had none.

The detailed arithmetic is in [the audit](legacy/what-i-would-change.md).

## What the pilot must prove

Within 12 weeks, find a product-price combination that reaches taste parity with the control, clears the price-intent gate, produces at least 20% identifiable eight-week repeat and has positive second-order CM2. Distribution does not widen if those conditions are not met.

## Category read

India is the centre of makhana supply. The APEDA-ICRIER report says India accounts for more than 90% of global production and Bihar contributes roughly 85% to 90% of Indian output. It also says official production and trade data remain limited, partly because makhana did not have a dedicated HS code. I have kept that caveat beside every market estimate rather than treating the category as precisely measured.

APEDA's November 2025 dashboard, citing CRISIL, placed the Indian market at roughly ₹8,000 to ₹8,500 crore in 2024-25 and projected ₹11,000 to ₹12,000 crore by 2029-30. The same dashboard reported sharp raw-material inflation, with average domestic makhana prices moving from ₹500 per kg in 2022 to ₹1,250 in 2025. The direction is useful; the numbers are market estimates, not audited category sales or a procurement quote.

This creates an attractive but awkward market. Consumer awareness, flavour innovation and quick delivery are expanding. At the same time, input volatility can destroy a low-priced pack before demand is even tested.

## Competition and the opening

The observed price ladder is already quite clear.

| Role | Examples observed | Read-through |
|---|---|---|
| Trial pack | Too Yumm about 16 g at ₹50 MRP; Farmley and Happilo 20 g at ₹50 MRP | ₹50 is an established trial price point |
| Core pack | MOM 52 g at ₹140; Mr Makhana 55 g at ₹150; Happilo 55 g at ₹139 | A ₹139 to ₹150 MRP for roughly 50 to 55 g is credible |
| Larger premium pack | Farmley 77 g at ₹239; Mr Makhana 80 g at ₹230 | Larger packs carry a visible premium |

Prices were observed on brand sites and BigBasket on 30 August 2026. Selling prices change often, so the file records both MRP and the observed discount.

The brands also show different routes into the category:

- Farmley began in B2B sourcing and later moved into consumer products. In a 2025 founder interview, the company said online channels still represented 75% of revenue while offline expansion was underway. This is self-reported, but it shows the advantage of learning online before building broad distribution.
- Mr Makhana's own history shows a narrow beginning in 2017, a production facility in 2018 and later expansion. The sequence matters more than the exact dates: product, process and distribution were built in stages.
- Too Yumm uses a broad snacking portfolio and a taste-first promise, with health cues such as no palm oil. It does not ask the buyer to choose between taste and better-for-you language.

The opening is not “another healthy snack.” It is a product that tastes familiar, states its nutrition plainly and arrives in the right pack for an evening desk or streaming occasion.

## Target customer and occasion

The initial target is a hypothesis, not a persona invented from a desk.

**Primary hypothesis:** urban professionals aged roughly 22 to 35 in Delhi NCR who already order snacks online and want crunch for the late-afternoon or evening desk/streaming occasion. They are curious about a lighter snack but will not forgive weak seasoning, a chewy texture or a misleading nutrition claim.

**Secondary hypothesis:** students and young professionals who will try a ₹50 pack but need a strong reason to move to a ₹149 pack.

The segment is deliberately behavioural. “Gen Z” alone does not explain an occasion, a channel or a reason to repeat.

**Pain-trigger hypothesis:** the buyer becomes open to switching when a 4 pm to 8 pm snack craving meets one of three frictions with the usual choice: it feels too heavy, the portion feels wrong or the label promise is hard to trust. The entry point is a familiar flavour, visible pack information and immediate availability. This is a question for interviews and paid behaviour, not a consumer fact.

Before the first commercial run I would run 60 interviews across three groups: regular makhana buyers, lapsed buyers and salty-snack buyers who rarely choose makhana. The questions are in the repository. No interview findings are fabricated here.

## Product and position

**Working promise:** familiar Indian snack flavour, crisp makhana and a label that tells the truth.

I would start with:

1. Chatpata Masala
2. Cream & Onion

Both are easy to understand without education. A hotter or more unusual flavour can be a limited test after the base product earns repeat.

The proposed architecture is:

| Pack | Working MRP | Job |
|---|---:|---|
| 20 g trial | ₹50 | Low-risk trial, office sampling conversion, add-on order |
| 50 g core | ₹149 | Main individual pack and unit-economics anchor |
| Four-pack D2C bundle | ₹499 | Mixed-flavour trial, gifting and owned feedback |

The final grammage and price must survive a monadic price test and manufacturer quotes. The product should not be called “high protein,” “low fat,” “gluten-free” or “healthy” until the final formula and lab report meet the applicable FSSAI conditions. Raw popped makhana nutrition is not proof of the finished seasoned product.

## Channel choice

Quick commerce is useful because snacking is immediate and impulse-led. Eternal reported Blinkit FY25 net order value of ₹22,371 crore across 424 million orders, with 1,301 stores at year end. Swiggy reported that Instamart reached 124 cities and 1,021 active stores in FY25. These platforms have reach, but brand-level commercial terms are private.

The plan therefore uses channel-cost ranges rather than a single “standard commission.”

| Channel | Role in the pilot | What it should prove |
|---|---|---|
| Office and community tasting | Fast product feedback | Blind taste, texture, flavour and price response |
| Selected premium stores | Physical discovery | Weekly velocity, reorder and expiry |
| Quick commerce | Convenience and repeat | Node-level velocity, promotion dependence and repeat |
| D2C bundles | Owned learning | Mixed-pack conversion, feedback and gifting demand |

D2C is not a plan to pull people away from a convenience app. It is a useful home for bundles and learning. Quick commerce should begin only after the product and price gates pass, and then in 40 to 60 nodes rather than 1,000.

## Conversion and recovery

The path is deliberately short:

`relevant occasion -> tasted or discovered -> paid trial -> core-pack purchase -> repeat -> bundle or referral`

Each break in the path needs a different response. A person who tastes but does not buy needs a price or product diagnosis. A buyer who never returns needs a taste, portion or occasion diagnosis. A buyer who purchases only under promotion is not treated as healthy repeat. An out-of-stock event is an operations failure, not a marketing failure.

The detailed recovery table and metrics are in [conversion-and-recovery.md](strategy/conversion-and-recovery.md).

## Twelve-week launch

### Weeks 1 to 2: prove the problem and price

- 60 structured interviews
- competitor pack teardown and label review
- price test of ₹129, ₹149 and ₹169 for the core pack, with each respondent shown one price
- three manufacturer quotes using the same specification
- FSSAI, HSN and claims review with the chosen manufacturer and advisers

### Weeks 3 to 4: prove the product

- two formulation rounds
- at least 120 blind tests against one established competitor
- record overall liking, crunch, seasoning, aftertaste and purchase intent
- reject a flavour that does not reach parity on overall liking

### Weeks 5 to 8: controlled sale

- pilot batch with traceable lots
- 20 to 30 selected stores plus office/community selling
- D2C mixed bundle for feedback and gifting
- weekly cohort and expiry review

### Weeks 9 to 12: quick-commerce cell test

- 40 to 60 NCR nodes, subject to commercial terms
- one controlled introductory offer, not a permanent discount
- compare exposed and non-exposed nodes where the platform allows it
- review velocity, repeat, contribution and stock age every two weeks

The full workplan is in [launch-plan.md](strategy/launch-plan.md).

## Gates

These are management rules for this pilot, not category benchmarks.

| Gate | Pass rule | Action if it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Blind taste | At least parity with the control on overall liking; no major texture defect | Reformulate before commercial inventory |
| Price | At least 35% definitely/probably buy at the selected price in the intended segment | Change pack, price or proposition |
| Early sell-through | Median node reaches 5 units per week by week 4 without a deep discount | Fix visibility or remove weak nodes |
| Repeat | At least 20% of identifiable first-time buyers reorder within 8 weeks | Diagnose taste, pack size and occasion |
| Economics | Second order CM2 is positive in the base commercial case | Renegotiate, reprice or stop paid acquisition |
| Quality | Complaints below 1.5% and no critical food-safety event | Stop the lot and follow the recall process |
| Inventory | At least 70% of a batch sold with 60% shelf life remaining | Reduce next batch and distribution breadth |

## Economics

The model starts from the consumer price and walks down to cash.

For each pack:

`net sales = consumer selling price / (1 + GST)`

`CM1 = net sales - trade deduction - brand promotion - product cost`

`CM2 = CM1 - fulfilment - variable marketing - samples/returns`

The base case uses a 50 g core pack at ₹149 MRP, a 5% consumer discount, 12% GST as a working assumption, a 30% channel deduction on ex-GST sales and ₹57.50 product cost. That product cost is an input to replace with a quote. It includes makhana, seasoning, packaging, conversion and inbound freight.

The workbook includes downside and upside cases for raw input, channel deduction, promotion, velocity and repeat. It also shows cash tied in inventory and receivables. Comparisons should be made on net order value and contribution, not on MRP GMV.

The pilot budget is capped at ₹30 lakh:

| Use | Cap |
|---|---:|
| Research, formulation and lab work | ₹3.5 lakh |
| Design, packaging plates and pilot setup | ₹3.0 lakh |
| Pilot inventory and deposits | ₹8.0 lakh |
| Sampling and field activation | ₹5.0 lakh |
| Quick-commerce listing, media and launch offers | ₹6.0 lakh |
| Core team and operating overhead | ₹3.0 lakh |
| Contingency | ₹1.5 lakh |

Every later tranche depends on the previous gate. A larger fundraise is a consequence of evidence, not a starting assumption.

## Measurement

The weekly dashboard should stay small:

- units and net sales by SKU, node and channel
- in-stock rate
- median units per node per week
- full-price and promoted velocity
- identifiable 4-week and 8-week repeat
- CM1 and CM2 per pack
- promotion as a share of net sales
- stock age, expiry and returns
- complaint rate and reason

Followers, impressions and total GMV are diagnostic at best. They do not replace repeat or contribution.

## Risks and controls

| Risk | Early signal | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Raw makhana inflation | Quote moves outside model range | Short quote validity, multiple suppliers, pack/price scenario ready |
| Weak crunch or seasoning | Taste score and complaint reasons | Blind test, moisture specification, retained samples |
| Permanent discount dependence | Full-price velocity collapses | Limit launch offer, read cohorts separately |
| Channel economics worsen | CM2 turns negative | Model actual terms by channel; pause weak nodes |
| Slow inventory | Batch sells late in shelf life | Smaller runs and tighter node count |
| Unsupported claim | Label or ad cannot be substantiated | Lab report and regulatory review before print |
| Supply concentration | Delay or inconsistent grade | Approved backup supplier and incoming QC |

## My conclusion

Makhana is not short of awareness anymore. The harder job is giving the buyer a reason to choose one pack twice while paying enough to absorb an expensive agricultural input and convenience-channel costs.

That changes the order of work. Taste and price come before reach. A narrow city cell comes before national distribution. The model follows the evidence instead of forcing the evidence to fit a large launch story.
